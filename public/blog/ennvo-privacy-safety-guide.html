Privacy and safety on Ennvo: a practical guide

A social platform should feel safe enough to be useful. This guide covers the practical steps you can take to protect your account, your content, and the people you talk to on Ennvo.

Start with your account

Your account is the front door to everything else. Use a strong, unique password and never share it with anyone. Ennvo will not ask for your password in a public comment, a story reply, or an unexpected message. If someone asks, treat it as a scam.

If you sign in on a shared device, sign out when you are finished. Keep your email account secure too, because it may be used to recover access to Ennvo.

Never send verification codes to another person. A code is proof that someone is trying to access your account, not a ticket for customer support.

Review your privacy settings

Open your account settings and review who can see your content and who can contact you. Choose the audience that matches how you actually use Ennvo. If you mostly share with friends, do not leave your account open to everyone by accident.

Remember that privacy settings are not a force field. Someone who can see a post can screenshot it. Share sensitive information only with people you trust, and prefer a private conversation over a public post when the topic is personal.

Think before you post

A useful rule is to ask three questions before publishing: Who can see this? Could it identify me or someone else? Would I be comfortable if it appeared again next year?

  • Avoid posting your home address, exact location, phone number, or identity documents.
  • Ask for permission before sharing a photo or video of another person.
  • Do not post someone else's private messages or personal information.
  • Be careful with photos that show a school, workplace, or daily routine.

Blocking and reporting

If an account or post makes you uncomfortable, open the account or content menu and choose the block or report option. Blocking stops that account from interacting with you. Reporting sends the issue to the Ennvo team for review.

You do not need to argue with someone before blocking them. Safety comes first. If the situation feels urgent or threatening, contact local emergency services as well as reporting it on the platform.

Stay alert to scams

Common scams ask for money, gift cards, passwords, or verification codes. Some pretend to be from Ennvo support. Others offer a prize, a job, or a relationship and then ask for personal information.

Slow down when a message creates urgency. Open Ennvo directly instead of following a link from a stranger. Check the account name carefully. If something feels wrong, stop the conversation and report it.

Safer calls and messages

Only call people you are comfortable speaking with. Remember that a call can be recorded by the other person, even if the app does not show a recording indicator. Do not share sensitive information during a call with someone you do not know well.

If a call or message becomes abusive, end the conversation and use the block and report tools. Keep evidence if you need to report a serious issue, but do not share other people's private data publicly.

What Ennvo collects and why

The Ennvo Privacy Policy explains what information is collected, how it is used, and the choices available to you. The short version is that account details, content, messages, and technical information are used to run the service, keep it secure, and support users.

You can contact support to ask about your data, request a correction, or ask for account deletion. Use the email address linked to your account so the team can verify the request.

If something goes wrong

  1. Stop interacting with the account or content.
  2. Block the account if you need to.
  3. Report the content or account.
  4. Change your password if you think your account was accessed.
  5. Contact support with details, screenshots if safe, and the account involved.

For platform safety concerns, use the Trust & Safety page or open a support ticket. For immediate danger, contact local authorities first.

The short version

Use a strong password, review your audience, share less personal information, block early, and report clearly. Privacy is not a single setting; it is a set of small choices made consistently.
